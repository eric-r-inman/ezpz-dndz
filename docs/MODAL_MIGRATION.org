#+title:     Modal migration plan
#+language:  en

Status: in progress on the =modal-migration-inline= branch.  This document
records the plan for moving the frontend away from modal dialogs, prompted by
external review feedback.  The foundation rename and the inline and panel tiers
are implemented there; =main= is untouched until the branch is reviewed and
merged.  Each phase lands as its own change set.

* Why step away from modals

The app is a live combat tracker: the encounter queue — initiative order, HP,
conditions, whose turn it is — is the one surface a GM needs visible at all
times, and every modal covers it.  Applying damage through a modal hides the
other creature cards; opening the Compendium hides the fight.  A tool whose
purpose is "less bookkeeping, more attention on the table" should not repeatedly
blank the table.

The supporting reasons:

- Modal flows cost open → act → close on actions that repeat dozens of times per
  session.
- The ~Modal~ ADT enforces one open modal, so switching tools mid-flow loses or
  hides in-progress work.
- Modals are the hardest common widget to make accessible (focus traps, escape
  handling, scroll locking, screen-reader announcement).
- On a tablet propped at the table, modals are especially clumsy.
- Current design guidance treats modals as a last resort for genuine
  interruptions, not as the default container for features.

* The tiered policy

Rather than a flat ban, each surface uses the least disruptive pattern that fits
it:

1. *Inline* on the creature card — for high-frequency, per-creature actions.
   Extends the existing inline-edit idiom (the AC readout).
2. *Popover* anchored to the triggering control — for small, pick-one
   interactions.
3. *Docked panel / drawer* beside the queue — for combat tools that need real
   estate but must not hide the queue.
4. *Route / full page* — for destinations: prep-time surfaces that do not need
   the encounter on screen at all.
5. *Toast + undo* — replacing blocking confirmations with reversibility.

A true modal remains acceptable only for a destructive, must-decide-now
interruption, and the toast + undo pattern should eliminate most of those.

* Mapping

Every modal the app started with, and its target pattern.

** Inline on the creature card

- =HpChange= (Damage / Heal / Temp HP) :: Converted as a card expansion, then
  re-homed twice: its trigger is in the Actions column and the editor opens in
  the drawer, targeting the active creature.  The ten-entry log shrinks to "last
  change + undo" in the editor; the full log lives in the dice panel.
- =Condition= :: Same journey as =HpChange= — column trigger, drawer panel,
  active-creature default, with a second button applying to the selection.  The
  biggest payoff of the migration: conditions get set while watching the fight.
- =Memo= (the row-three note) :: Clicking the icon becomes an in-place text
  input; no surface at all.
- =Note= (the name note) :: Identical treatment, completing the existing
  inline-edit idiom.
- =Timer= :: Originally mapped as a popover, but converted with this group so
  every card-button editor shares one UX; it may still shrink to a popover once
  that tier's anchored container exists.
- =SaveChain= :: Originally mapped as a right-rail panel; it shares the
  column-plus-drawer home of =HpChange= and =Condition=, which is that panel on
  the other side of the queue.
- =Initiative= :: Originally mapped as a popover anchored to the clicked
  initiative badge; it took the column-plus-drawer treatment instead, since its
  roll-and-sort batch is too wide for an anchored container.  The card's init
  circle still aims the editor at its own creature.

** Popover anchored to the trigger

- =AbilitySave= :: Already receives click coordinates and behaves like a popup;
  formalize it as an anchored popover (roll / advantage / disadvantage) outside
  the modal ADT.
- =Duplicate= :: First reclassified as its existing dropdown menu, then promoted
  to a drawer panel beside =Replace= (the Quick Add picker's replace mode, given
  its own surface) — both with apply-to-selected scopes and recent-applies logs.
- =QuickAdd= :: Mapped as an anchored popover under the button; landed as a
  drawer panel with the same search box and capped-height scroll, since it would
  have been the tier's only member.

** Docked panel / drawer

- =Dice= :: The strongest panel candidate — a GM plausibly wants it persistently
  open.  Mapped as a right-rail dock beside the stat block; landed in the
  Actions column's drawer with it, which means the two take turns rather than
  sitting side by side.
- =Treasure= (the roller) :: Rolls against the current queue's enemies; a drawer
  keeps them visible while results generate.
- =CrCalculator= :: Reads the live queue by design; as a panel, the difficulty
  updates visibly as creatures are added or removed.
- =RandomEncounter= :: Its entire output is creatures appearing in the queue; a
  drawer means seeing them land.
- =SpellList= :: Mapped as a right-rail drawer, landed as a drop-down under the
  queue's spellcaster strip, so the reminder naming the casters and the
  reference listing their spells are one affordance.
- =Save= :: Mapped as a three-way split — a one-click quick-save button with a
  toast, a small "save as" drawer, and a =/saves= management route.  Only the
  drawer landed, carrying the whole form; the quick-save button and the route
  are still ahead.

All of these landed in one frame rather than a drawer per tier: the far-left
Actions column gathers the encounter-scoped triggers, and the panel beside it
shows whichever was pressed.  =QuickAdd= went there too — the popover the tier
above mapped it to would have been the only one of its kind — and so did the
pinned stat block, the XP scope, and the Reset / Clear confirmations, which is
what let the two right-hand panes go away entirely.  =View.Panel= carries the
shared chrome, with its shell fields lined up against =View.Modal='s, so moving
a feature between the two is mostly a matter of swapping the wrapper.

** Route / full page

- =Compendium= (browser) :: The flagship conversion: a =/compendium= section.
  It is a database UI, not an interruption, and its selection-plus-detail layout
  wants the space.  Landed: the browser lives only in its own tab now, opened
  with nothing selected, and the in-page modal that used to be its fallback is
  gone.
- =CompendiumEdit= :: =/compendium/edit/:id=.  The deepest form in the app; a
  page gives it room and normal browser behavior (back button, refresh-safe).
- =CompendiumPaste= :: A step under the compendium route; paste → parse preview
  → save is a linear flow suited to a page.
- =GroupEdit= :: Nested compendium route; group building is prep work.
- =LoreEdit= :: Nested compendium route.
- =TreasureTable= (the table editor, distinct from the roller) :: Prep-time
  editing of reference data; the roller drawer links to it.
- =Load= :: Mapped as a =/saves= management page (rename, delete, server/local)
  with a "recent saves" popover for the common case; landed as a drawer panel
  beside =Save=, since the two share a shape and splitting them across tiers
  would have been the odder half of the pair.
- =SaveCompendium= / =LoadCompendium= :: Fold both into the compendium route as
  its import/export screen; they share the file-management shape of encounter
  save/load.

** Toast + undo

The Reset and Clear confirmations, and the overwrite confirmation inside Save,
become non-blocking toasts with an Undo action.  Faster at the table, and it
removes the last must-answer-now surfaces — which is what makes the policy
workable without an escape hatch.

* Sequencing

1. Rename the ~Modal~ ADT to a ~Surface~ ADT with ~Inline~ / ~Popover~ / ~Panel~
   / ~Route~ variants.  Pure refactor, no behavior change; every later phase
   builds on it.
2. Convert the combat-critical four — =HpChange=, =Condition=, =SaveChain=,
   =Dice= — which deliver nearly all the at-the-table value.
3. Move the compendium family (browser, editor, paste, groups, lore,
   import/export) to routes.
4. Sweep the small popovers and the toast + undo conversions last.

The =Update/*= modules barely change throughout: this is almost entirely
=View/*= and ~Model.modal~ plumbing, which is what the frontend layering (see
[[file:ARCHITECTURE.org][ARCHITECTURE.org]]) was built to absorb.  The panel-based layout is also
effectively the second UI layout the domain/view split was designed to enable.

* Engineering plan

How the phases above translate into concrete changes.  Rollback strategy: all
work lands on the =modal-migration-inline= branch, pushed to GitHub, so
switching between the old and new UI is a =git switch= (plus =just dev= to
rebuild) in either direction.  The branch merges to =main= via squash — its
published polish-chain history collapses to the one-commit-per-PR shape the
conventions ask for.

** Foundation: the Surface rename

A mechanical, behavior-preserving rename, verified entirely by the compiler:

- ~type Modal~ becomes ~type Surface~, and each ~ModalX~ constructor becomes
  ~SurfaceX~.
- ~Model.modal~ becomes ~Model.surface~; ~ModalLens~ / ~mapModal~ become
  ~SurfaceLens~ / ~mapSurface~.

Three things deliberately keep their names.  ~Ui.ModalChrome~ and the =.modal*=
CSS classes describe the floating-window chrome, which remains real for every
surface still rendered as an overlay.  The =View.Modal.*= module namespace
shrinks one module at a time as features convert — each conversion moves its
view to the namespace matching its new pattern (=View/Inline/*=, later
=View/Popover/*= and =View/Panel/*=), so the module path always tells the truth
about how the surface renders.

The tier a variant belongs to is expressed by where its view is mounted (the
overlay stack in =Main.appShell=, a card expansion, a rail panel, a route), not
by a reified kind enum — a ~kindOf~ function would be a second copy of the
mapping in this document, drifting the moment a variant converts.

** Inline tier mechanics

The four inline conversions keep their Ui records, Msg families, and Update
modules byte-identical; only the rendering moves.

- *State threading.* =View.Card.view= gains a context record (built in
  =View.Workspace=) carrying the open surface, the selected-creature count, the
  condition presets, the queue's creature names, and the newest HP-log entry —
  the model fragments the transplanted bodies already consumed.
- *Expansion slot.* When the open surface targets a card's creature, the card
  renders a =.creature-card__inline= section under row 3 with the transplanted
  form body (HP change, condition) and a header close button.  The
  one-open-at-a-time invariant carries over unchanged: opening any surface
  replaces the previous one, so at most one card is ever expanded.
- *Compact editors.* Memo and note get a one-row strip below the card rows
  (title, short input, ✓ save) instead of the full expansion.  Their triggers
  stay exactly where they are, highlighted while the editor is open — a row must
  never reflow around an editor, and the visible trigger is what makes the
  cancel toggle reachable.  Enter or ✓ commits; Escape or the trigger cancels.
  Deliberately no blur-commit (unlike the AC edit): committing on blur would
  fire before a cancel click could land.
- *Toggle-to-close.* The button that opened an inline editor closes it again
  unchanged — once the editor lives on the card, its trigger reads as a toggle,
  and re-clicking it is the natural cancel gesture.  This replaces the footer
  Cancel buttons, which the editors therefore drop.
- *Escape coverage.* The subscription table in =Main= gains cases for the three
  converted surfaces that previously had no Esc handling (HP change, condition,
  memo), closing an inconsistency the modal era left behind.
- *The HP log.* The card expansion shows only the newest entry with its undo
  button.  The full ten-entry list moves to the dice panel through a shared
  =View/HpLog= module, so the rendering exists once.

** Later tiers, briefly

- *Popovers* get a shared anchored-container helper (=View/Popover=) that
  positions against the triggering control.  =AbilitySave= is the tier's only
  remaining member, and already carries click coordinates.
- *Routes* extend =Route= with the compendium family and =/saves=; the existing
  standalone-page pattern (=View/Page/*=) is the template.
- *Toast + undo* replaces the =PendingControl= confirmations last.

** Verification

Every step must pass ~elm make~ and ~elm-test~ before it lands; behavioral
phases also get a manual =just dev= pass.  The four inline features have no
view-layer tests today — their domain logic (=HpChange=, condition durations) is
covered by the existing pure suites, which must stay green untouched.
